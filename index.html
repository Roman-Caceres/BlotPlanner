How to use

The planner assumes a Bio-Rad Precision Plus Protein Dual Color ladder (#1610374). If you run a different ladder its band sizes will not match, and the positions will be wrong — see the caution at the end of this section.

Planning a blot

  1. Gel. Set the acrylamide percentage and the resolving gel length in millimetres — measured from the top of the resolving gel, below the stacking gel you cut away, down to the dye front.
  2. Targets. Add each protein by its observed molecular weight, taken from the antibody datasheet. Add a loading control from the same subcellular fraction as your target.
  3. Cuts. Press Auto-place cuts. The tool puts a cut midway between targets, keeping your band clearance, and refuses to create strips below your minimum height. Drag any cut to override it.
  4. Read the Checks panel. This is the part worth your attention — it flags targets too close to separate, cuts that would bisect a band, and same-host primaries sharing a strip.

Before you actually cut

  1. Run the gel and transfer as normal, then Ponceau-stain the membrane and photograph it.
  2. Lay a ruler on the photo and measure each visible ladder band, from the top of the resolving gel.
  3. Switch Position source to Calibrated and type those distances in. Two bands minimum; more is better. Press Fit working range.
  4. Check R². Above 0.99 is good. If it is low, untick bands outside the gel's resolving range and refit — ladders stop being log-linear at the extremes.
  5. Now re-read the cut positions. Mark them with pencil on the membrane edge and cut against a ruler with a fresh blade. Keep an orientation notch in a known corner.
Using a different ladder? The band sizes throughout — including the rows in the calibration table — are those of Precision Plus Dual Color. With another ladder (PageRuler, for instance) the labels will not correspond to your bands, and typing measurements against them will produce confidently wrong positions. Support for other ladders is planned; say which one you use and it moves up the list.

Position sources

Band positions come from one of three sources. Only the last is grounded in your gel, and the tool always shows which one is active.

1 · Model — an estimate

Rf is taken as linear in log10(MW) across the gel's resolving range, the standard SDS-PAGE relationship, anchored so the top of the range sits at Rf 0.15 and the bottom at Rf 0.85, then passed through a logistic squash (k = 4) so proteins outside the range compress toward the well or the dye front instead of leaving the gel. The ranges used — 15% = 10–40, 12% = 20–80, 10% = 40–120, 8% = 60–200 kDa — are conventional rule-of-thumb figures for handcast Tris-glycine gels.

This mode is a construction, not vendor data. It exists to give you a sane starting layout before you have a gel to measure.

2 · Bio-Rad chart — real vendor data, wrong ladder

Rf values measured by pixel analysis of Bio-Rad's published Criterion Tris-HCl migration chart, normalised as (band − well bottom) / (gel bottom − well bottom), across all eleven columns. Between tabulated bands the tool interpolates linearly in log10(MW); beyond them it extrapolates and says so. A band missing from a column means Bio-Rad's chart does not show it — it has run off that gel.

Three caveats that matter:
  • Bio-Rad states these are Precision Plus Protein unstained standards. #1610374 Dual Color is prestained. Dual Color is engineered to land at its printed MW on a Laemmli gel so the two should be close — but that is an inference, not a measurement.
  • The chart is for precast midi Criterion gels, not handcast mini Tris-glycine. Rf normalises out gel length but not chemistry or casting.
  • Positions were read off a schematic drawing, not densitometry.

The chart also has no 8% or 12% column, so those recipes map to the nearest available 7.5% and 12.5%.

3 · Calibrated — your gel

Discards every model above and fits distance = A + B·log10(MW) by ordinary least squares to distances you measured off your own Ponceau, reporting R² and residuals, with per-band control over what enters the fit.

Real ladders stop being log-linear at the extremes — the largest and smallest bands compress. Including them drags the fitted line and worsens the prediction exactly where your targets sit. If R² is low, untick the bands outside the gel's resolving range and refit.

This is the only mode grounded in the membrane you are about to cut. Use it before you score anything.

Cut placement

Each target gets a protected zone: its band extent plus your band-clearance margin above and below. Targets whose zones overlap cannot be separated and are merged into one strip. Cuts are then placed midway between consecutive zones, and any cut that would leave a strip shorter than your minimum height is dropped.

Cut positions are reported in millimetres from the top of the resolving gel, and also relative to the nearest ladder band — easier to use at the bench than a ruler origin. Drag any cut on the gel to override it.

Ladder

Bio-Rad Precision Plus Protein Dual Color Standards #1610374: ten bands at 250, 150, 100, 75, 50, 37, 25, 20, 15 and 10 kDa. The 75 and 25 kDa bands are pink, the other eight blue. Band identities were confirmed against the vendor product image.

Loading control provenance

Every preset molecular weight was read from that antibody's own Cell Signaling Technology product page, from the "MW (kDa)" specification field, and is quoted verbatim. Each button's tooltip carries the catalog number the value came from, so any of them can be rechecked at cellsignal.com/products/<cat>/datasheet. None were written from memory.

CST's own summary table in their loading-control blog post disagrees with their product pages in three places: it lists β-Tubulin at 35 kDa and Profilin-1 at 9/5 kDa, where the datasheets say 55 and 15; and it gives α-Actinin's catalog number as 64887, which does not resolve — the real product is #6487 (100 kDa). This tool follows the product pages in every case.

Where a datasheet lists two bands — Lamin B1 68 and 45, Lamin A/C 70 and 28, Caveolin-1 21 and 24 — the preset uses the full-length species and the tooltip names the other, because a cleavage product you didn't plan for is a good way to lose a strip.

Controls are grouped by subcellular fraction. Pick one from the same fraction as your target — a cytoplasmic control says nothing about loading in a nuclear prep.

Observed vs sequence MW

Enter the observed molecular weight — what the vendor says you will actually see on a blot — not the weight calculated from the sequence. Observed weights shift with glycosylation, phosphorylation, multimerisation, species and cell type. Always prefer the number on the datasheet of the antibody actually in your freezer. For proteins that run as a doublet or a smear, enter both bounds and the tool will protect the whole span.

Privacy

Your experiment never leaves your browser. Targets, molecular weights, antibodies, gel settings and calibration measurements are all computed locally and stored only in this browser's own storage. There is no server to send them to, no account, and no sign-in. Clearing your browser data deletes them; the Export JSON button is how you keep a copy.

The hosted site may count anonymous page views so it is possible to tell whether anyone is using the tool. That counter records a visit and nothing else — no IP address, no cookies, and nothing about your work. It never runs on a downloaded copy or when the installed app is used offline. If you would rather not be counted at all, download index.html and open it directly; it is the complete tool and makes no network requests whatsoever.
